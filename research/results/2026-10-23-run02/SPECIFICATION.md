# Preregistered operational design

Written before financial simulation; no outcome-driven parameter search. Main window: all complete calendar years 2022–2025. Robustness windows: 2022–2023, 2024–2025, and 2026 YTD through the last complete acquired session. Calendar windows are chosen before inspecting strategy returns. These are overlapping market samples, not independent replications.

## Implemented rules and controlled differences

Inspected: `backend/routers/recommendations.py`, `backend/routers/cashflow.py`, `backend/market_trends.py`, `backend/services.py`, `backend/market_data.py`, ETL, migrations and live risk-function definitions. The database's deployed adjusted-return view differs from the older raw-close migration; its definition is archived. `docs/research_experiment_spec.md` was absent. PDFs were found outside the repository and remain unchanged.

Monthly buffer B = max(0, income − mandatory expenses − reserve). Discretionary expenses are recorded but are not subtracted by the implemented application. The application subtracts the configured reserve from each monthly surplus; we preserve this convention rather than reinterpret it as an already-funded wealth reserve. Tier capacity c = .25/.50/.75. Risk aversion a = 1−c. All policies use the actual daily score, mean(r) − a[sd(r)+CVaR95(r)] − momentum penalty, up to 252 returns and at least 120. They use inverse-volatility sizing 1/sd(r)^a, exclude held stocks, select at most three stocks from distinct sectors and floor whole-share quantities. The baseline ranking score is identical within a tier; only the risk exposure ordering and active policy restrictions differ.

| Factor | Affordability Only | Risk Only | No Feedback | Full IntelliVest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proposed budget | Bc | brokerage cash × c | Bc | Bc × feedback multiplier |
| Brokerage cash constraint | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Existing-stock exclusion | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Sector exposure ordering | disabled (all weights zero) | lower exposure first | lower exposure first | lower exposure first |
| Fixed policy | no sector blocks | initial sector blocks | initial sector blocks | initial policy, then updates |
| Dynamic breach response | none | none | none | current dominant-sector block; multiplier .5 for VaR OR volatility, .25 for both, 1 otherwise |

The initial portfolio is empty, so initial policy has multiplier 1 and no blocked sectors. The actual application has **no independent hard pretrade HHI gate**: HHI restrictions are feedback. We do not invent one for C. B and C maintain ordinary exposure-aware selection, but suppress every subsequent breach-driven policy change. A disables exposure ordering as part of the intended risk-control ablation. All four retain tier-specific stock risk ranking, an explicit common factor. Thus this tests household gating, exposure ordering and feedback; it cannot identify an absent fixed HHI gate. No Feedback is operationally meaningful, not Full under another name.

## Capital and schedule

Nine explicitly synthetic scenarios cross three monthly INR situations with all three risk tiers: low (income 45,000; mandatory 30,000; discretionary 3,000; reserve 10,000; buffer 5,000), medium (100,000; 50,000; 10,000; 30,000; buffer 20,000), high (250,000; 90,000; 30,000; 60,000; buffer 100,000). Starting brokerage cash is INR 100,000 for every policy and profile, representing pre-existing savings outside the protected household reserve. No starting holdings. On the first observed session of each month, B is transferred to every policy's account. B does not grow with unspent brokerage cash. Identical contributions allow Risk Only to spend accumulated savings beyond current household capacity without negative cash. Orders are fully accepted by the simulated investor; no behavioral acceptance model.

Monthly decisions use the first session's completed close; orders execute at the **next observed session close**, with 10 bp proportional fee and 5 bp adverse price slippage. Whole-share quantities are set using decision-date nominal prices, capped at execution by both cash and (where enabled) remaining household capacity including fees. Unexecuted quantities are cancelled, never rolled forward. Missing execution bars cancel the order; missing valuation bars for held stocks fail the run. No manufactured bars, interpolation, forward-filled returns or zero-return substitutes. A monthly batch is consumed once. App freshness/idempotency UI logic is replaced by this fixed schedule; no live recommendation history is read. No automatic sells: the actual feedback changes future buy recommendations, not holdings. Turnover is consequently one-way purchase turnover.

## Corporate actions, risk and evaluation

Ranking/estimation uses provider adjusted closes consistently. Execution and valuation use nominal share prices reconstructed from split-normalized Yahoo OHLC using the cumulative later splits in the downloaded horizon. Splits modify share counts at session start; fractional split entitlements are liquidated as cash-in-lieu at that session's nominal close. Dividends go to pre-existing shares on ex-date, approximating unknown payment dates. Demergers and unreconciled rights/identity changes must be excluded before replay. Retroactively revised vendor series and current sectors remain limitations; they are not point-in-time data vintages. Never use later returns in an indicator.

At each daily close after execution, estimate current-weight hypothetical portfolio adjusted returns over the last 252 aligned daily returns, including zero cash return, and 30-return volatility. The deployed SQL inspection established parametric VaR95 fraction = 1.645sd30 (no mean subtraction), enabled only with a complete 30-return window; this exact rule supersedes a conventional 252-day parametric formula. CVaR uses inclusive returns <= the linearly interpolated fifth percentile and requires 252 aligned returns. Breaches: parametric VaR/equity > configured .10; daily sd30 > .03; equity-only sector HHI > .25. These are application defaults shared across tiers, not calibrated to outcomes. The most exposed sector is blocked for Full on an HHI breach. Feedback is recomputed daily and takes effect at the next eligible monthly recommendation. Response latency is measured separately from whether holdings actually correct. A restricted proposal may have no trade effect.

Realized time-weighted daily return r_t = E_t/(E_(t−1)+C_t)−1, because contributions occur before that day's market movement; first day E_(t−1)=initial cash. Dividends, fees and slippage are internal. Cumulative return = product(1+r)−1; annualization uses 252/n sessions; Sharpe = sqrt(252)(mean(r)−.04/252)/sd(r); sample sd. Drawdown uses the cumulative time-weighted wealth index including initial 1, not nominal contributed equity. Realized VaR and CVaR use the same 95% daily convention over the entire evaluation window, distinct from rolling estimates. Mean HHI excludes dates with no equity (undefined); empty portfolios do not breach. Breach frequency and duration use observed sessions, with unresolved terminal episodes right-censored. Cash interest is zero. Cost sensitivity uses (fee,slippage) bp = (0,0),(10,5),(25,10) on the primary three profiles; no tax/tax-lot simulation.

## Statistical analysis and metrics

Decision violation rate = proposals exceeding B / decisions. Overspend = max(0,proposed notional−B), INR; report all-decision mean, conditional mean, and max. Surplus utilization = executed purchase cost including fee / total supplied B; also report monthly utilization and idle capacity max(0,B−executed cost). No-purchase frequency uses monthly execution outcomes, not empty individual order rows. Average cash allocation = daily cash/equity. Turnover = total executed purchase notional / mean daily equity (not annualized); fees and slippage separately. Ending equity, contributions, gain E_end−E_initial−sum(C), realized gains (zero absent sales/cash-in-lieu), cost basis and unrealized gains are separately available. HHI = sum(sector equity weight²), no cash sector. Allocation drift = one-half L1 change in equity sector weights between consecutive closes. Stability = Jaccard similarity of selected ticker sets on consecutive monthly decisions, empty/empty = 1. Repeated breach = breached metric on two consecutive observations; incident episodes counted separately from breached days. Policy adjustments count effective multiplier/block changes, not repeated identical alerts. Changed decisions compare Full with No Feedback monthly ticker/quantity vectors; same-state counterfactual captures direct feedback effects independently of divergent histories. Response time to first subsequent **different** proposal is recorded per breach episode, with unresolved episodes censored, not assigned zero.

Report paired policy differences by profile and window. Profile combinations are design sensitivity, not random participants; overlapping windows are not independent. A fixed seed 20261023 and paired circular 20-session moving-block bootstrap (2,000 draws) quantify conditional daily-return mean-difference uncertainty only, preserving shared dates between policies. Intervals are not population efficacy claims; no daily-observation independent t-tests or invented p-values. Three contrast families: Full–Risk Only, Full–Affordability Only, Full–No Feedback; Holm correction would apply if formal testing were justified, but the design uses descriptive intervals, not significance declarations. Momentum ablation zeros only actual tier momentum coefficients, preserves every other rule, and is reported separately from the four principal policies.

## Separately labelled feedback stress extension

The first 41-security execution showed identical Full and No Feedback decisions in the principal empty-account profiles, despite policy updates. This null result is retained in the run history. Before the final execution, a security identity audit excluded SHRIRAMFIN because its pre-merger SRTRANSFIN identity had not been reconciled, yielding 40 securities. No return outcome was used to select the exclusion. A secondary concentrated-start experiment was added explicitly to probe feedback activation, not to replace the null primary comparison: 90% of the same INR 100,000 initial wealth is targeted to the admitted stock with the highest sample volatility over the last 252 pre-start adjusted returns, chosen without post-start data; whole shares at the last pre-start nominal close, remainder cash. Low/conservative, medium/moderate and high/aggressive profiles use the same starting position for all four policies. Initial risk state is evaluated from pre-start history; Risk Only and No Feedback freeze those initial multiplier/sector restrictions, Full updates thereafter, and Affordability Only ignores them. This targeted mechanism stress is not an unbiased robustness sample, and its outcomes cannot substitute for a failed central H3 comparison.

Only the audit process connects to PostgreSQL, with server-enforced read-only transactions and market-only SELECTs. Replay reads CSV files and keeps all investor state in memory. It never calls application mutation services, creates users or writes research portfolios into the database.
